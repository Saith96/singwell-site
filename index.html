Why Singlark

Feedback on your voice, not on the song

Most people who sing for fun never get feedback. Lessons are expensive, and karaoke apps score the song, not the singer.

Know your voice in two minutes

A check-up finds your lowest and highest notes, how well you match a pitch, how steady you hold it and your longest note. Bass to soprano.

A workout for your range

About five minutes of warm-ups, long notes, steps, jumps and scales, placed in your own range, with every note graded.

Gentle and private

How it works

Two minutes to start, five a day to grow

  1. Take the check-up

    Sing a few notes. Singlark finds your range, your voice type and how well you hold a pitch.

  2. Do the daily workout

    Follow a short routine built around your range and your weakest score, with live feedback on every note.

  3. Hear your progress

    Each check-up keeps a short clip, so you can compare your first one with today, and watch your range grow.

Questions

Frequently asked

Do I need to be a good singer?

No. Singlark is for anyone who sings for fun: in the shower, in a choir or on stage. The check-up shows where you are today and the workout starts there.

Is my voice recorded or uploaded?

Your voice is analysed on your phone and never uploaded. Check-up clips are kept on your phone so you can hear your progress, and you can delete everything in Settings.

Singlark doesn't hear me. What can I do?

Check that the microphone is allowed in your phone's Settings, then sing a little closer to the phone in a quiet room. Headphones help, because the app then doesn't hear its own reference notes.

Is it free?

The check-up and free practice are free. Singlark Plus is a monthly or yearly subscription with a free week on the yearly plan.

My throat feels tired.

Stop and rest. Singlark is a practice tool, not medical advice. If hoarseness or pain lasts more than two weeks, see a doctor or a voice specialist.
